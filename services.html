Stage 1 Remapping

Stage 1 is a software remap for a standard vehicle. No parts are changed, just the software in the ECU.

What a Stage 1 remap does

Every engine leaves the factory set up to suit lots of different countries, fuel types and emissions rules, so most turbo engines have more to give. We read the software from your ECU, adjust the fuelling, boost and timing for your engine, and write it back.

What you'll notice

The car pulls harder through the gears, overtaking is easier and towing feels less of a struggle. On a lot of diesels you'll also see better mpg on a steady run.

Original file kept

We save your original software before we change anything, so the vehicle can go back to standard whenever you want.

Time on site

Most Stage 1 remaps are done through the OBD port in one to two hours. Some ECUs need bench or boot tuning, which takes a bit longer.

Stage 2 Remapping

Stage 2 takes things further for vehicles that already have performance parts fitted.

Who it's for

If you've fitted an uprated intercooler, induction kit, downpipe or performance exhaust, the engine needs software to match. A Stage 2 map is written around those parts.

How we do it

We check what's fitted, tune the software to suit and test it on the road, so you get the most from your upgrades without pushing the engine past safe limits.

Gearbox and clutch

More torque means more load on the drivetrain. We'll tell you up front if your clutch or gearbox needs looking at, and we can tune automatic and DSG gearboxes to match.

EGR Solutions

EGR solutions & EGR delete

A sticking or blocked EGR valve is one of the most common reasons a diesel runs rough, smokes or drops into limp mode.

What the EGR valve does

It feeds some of the exhaust gas back into the engine to bring emissions down. That gas is full of soot, and over time it clogs the valve, the cooler and the inlet.

Signs of an EGR problem

Engine light on, flat spots when you pull away, rough idle, black smoke, limp mode and higher fuel use.

Our EGR solutions

We read the codes and check live data to find out what's actually going on, then repair, clean or recalibrate the system. We also offer EGR delete software.

DPF Solutions

DPF solutions & DPF delete

A blocked diesel particulate filter brings on the DPF light, limp mode and poor performance.

What a DPF does

The diesel particulate filter catches soot in the exhaust and burns it off every so often. This is called a regen, and it needs a decent run at speed to finish.

Why they block

Lots of short journeys stop the regen from finishing, so the soot keeps building. A bad pressure sensor, temperature sensor, EGR fault or injector problem can cause it too.

Signs of a DPF problem

DPF light on, loss of power, limp mode, the fan running after you switch off and a hot smell from the exhaust.

Our DPF solutions

We check the soot level and sensors and work out why it isn't clearing. Where it's safe we run a forced regen and fix the fault behind it. We also offer DPF delete software.

AdBlue Solutions

AdBlue solutions & AdBlue delete

An AdBlue fault puts a countdown on the dash, and when it hits zero the vehicle may not start again.

What AdBlue does

AdBlue is sprayed into the exhaust before the SCR catalyst, where it turns NOx into nitrogen and water.

What goes wrong

The tank, pump, heater, injector and sensors all have to work together. Crystallised AdBlue, a failed heater or pump, bad fluid and sensor faults are what we see most.

Don't leave the countdown

Once the countdown starts, it's worth getting it looked at before it runs out. Once it does, a lot of vehicles won't restart.

Our AdBlue solutions

We diagnose the whole system where the vehicle is parked, from the pump and heater to the sensors and software, and get you back on the road. We also offer AdBlue delete software.

NOx Solutions

NOx solutions & NOx delete

A failed NOx sensor can bring on warning lights, limp mode and AdBlue errors.

What NOx sensors do

Vehicles with AdBlue have a NOx sensor either side of the catalyst. They tell the ECU whether the system is cleaning the exhaust properly.

Why they fail

They live in hot, sooty exhaust gas, so they wear out. Wiring faults and AdBlue dosing problems can throw the same codes, which is why we test before replacing anything.

Our NOx solutions

We confirm the fault with live data, so you don't pay for a sensor you didn't need, and price the job once we know what's wrong. We also offer NOx delete software.

Advanced Diagnostics

Our dealer-level diagnostic equipment talks to every module on the vehicle, not just the engine.

What we check

Engine, gearbox, ABS, airbag, body and emissions modules. We read and explain the fault codes, then check live data such as sensor readings, boost, fuel pressure and DPF soot load to confirm what's causing them.

Why it matters

A fault code tells you where to look, not always what's broken. Testing properly first means you only pay for the parts and work the vehicle actually needs.

When to book

Any warning light, limp mode, loss of power, rough running, or a fault that keeps coming back.

ECU Coding

Coding changes settings inside a control unit's existing software. It doesn't rewrite the software itself, that's programming.

When it's needed

Many modern vehicles need new parts registering before they work properly, such as a new battery on BMW and VAG models, or new injectors on many diesels, which need their individual codes entering.

Retrofits and settings

Fitted a factory part like a towbar, lights or a reversing camera? It has to be coded in. We can also change features the vehicle already supports, such as start-stop, lighting and comfort settings.

Check with us

What can be coded depends on the make, model and year, so send us your reg and what you're after.

ECU Programming

A new, used or refurbished ECU won't run your engine straight out of the box. It needs the right software and has to be matched to the vehicle.

What's involved

We load the correct software for your engine, match the ECU to the vehicle's VIN and immobiliser so it will start, then run any adaptations or relearns and clear the fault codes.

Software updates

Manufacturers release updated ECU software to fix known running faults. Where an update is available for your vehicle, we can install it.

Diagnose first

Programming won't fix a wiring fault or a failed sensor, so we confirm the ECU really is the problem before replacing or reprogramming it.

ECU & TCU Cloning

Cloning copies the full contents of your original ECU or TCU onto a replacement unit with the same part number.

Why clone instead of program

The clone gets the same software, immobiliser data, VIN and adaptations as your original. That means no new keys, no dealer coding and no immobiliser problems. The vehicle starts and drives exactly as it did before.

When it's used

When an ECU or gearbox control unit has failed, through water damage or an internal fault, but its memory can still be read. A good used unit with a matching part number is usually all you need.

TCU Tuning

Automatic and DSG gearboxes have their own control unit, the TCU, with its own torque limits and shift maps.

Why it's needed

After a remap the engine can make more torque than the gearbox software allows. The TCU then cuts power or the box can slip. Tuning the TCU raises those limits to match.

What else changes

Gear changes can be made quicker and firmer, shift points adjusted, and kickdown made more responsive, so the gearbox works with the remap rather than against it.

Speed Limiter Removal

Lots of ex-fleet and lease vans and cars come with a top speed limit set in the ECU, often around 56 to 70mph.

How it works

The limit, known as Vmax, is stored in the engine software, so it can be raised to a new figure or taken back to the vehicle's factory setting. No parts are added or removed.

Who it's for

Mostly buyers of ex-fleet vans and cars that were limited by their previous owner. Send us your reg and we'll confirm what can be done on your vehicle.
